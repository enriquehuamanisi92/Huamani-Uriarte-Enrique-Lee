# PROCIENCIA Application and Expected Peruvian Impact

## 1. What PROCIENCIA is

PROCIENCIA is Peru's *Programa Nacional de Investigación Científica y Estudios Avanzados* (National Program for Scientific Research and Advanced Studies), operating within the national science, technology, and innovation system under CONCYTEC. According to its official institutional description, PROCIENCIA is a specialized public fund that attracts, manages, and channels national and international resources for science, technology, and technological innovation. Its objectives include strengthening national research capacity, generating knowledge with practical value, supporting advanced human capital, and promoting scientific and technological transfer in Peru.

Official sources:

- PROCIENCIA institutional profile: https://prociencia.gob.pe/nosotros/
- Peruvian government institutional portal: https://www.gob.pe/prociencia
- PROCIENCIA funding-call overview: https://www.gob.pe/14464-que-hacemos-concursos-convocados-por-prociencia

## 3. Evidence and wording policy

This repository distinguishes four states:

1. **Submitted:** the proposal was registered in the application system.
2. **Eligible or under evaluation:** used only if an official record confirms that stage.
3. **Selected:** used only after publication of an official award decision.
4. **Funded:** used only after the applicable agreement and funding conditions are formalized.

At present, this repository claims only **submitted**. The word *Advanced* refers to the modality displayed in the submission record; it is not interpreted as an evaluation result. Screenshots supplied by the applicant should be preserved in the private project record with date, source URL, and original filename. ## 4. Why the application strengthens the doctoral project

The PROCIENCIA submission connects the doctoral research with a competitive national applied-research pathway. It demonstrates that the proposal is framed not only as a classroom exercise but also as a potential scientific-technological project with defined deliverables, institutional participation, technological maturity goals, and expected public value.

This does not replace academic evaluation. The doctoral contribution must still be supported by an original research gap, an auditable literature review, rigorous temporal-spatial validation, transparent negative or null results, and ethical governance.

## 5. Expected application in the Peruvian context if funded

If selected and funded, the project would move from the current synthetic proof of concept toward controlled validation in Peru through the following pathway:

1. **Institutional authorization:** establish agreements, ethics clearance, and lawful access to authorized police, census, cartographic, and municipal data.
2. **Peruvian data integration:** harmonize complaint categories, INEI indicators, official territorial units, and locally available urban-environment variables.
3. **Comas laboratory validation:** evaluate forecasting performance, calibration, spatial transfer, temporal drift, and territorial disparities using real aggregated observations.
4. **Responsible prototype:** produce an access-controlled, aggregate risk-mapping prototype for research and preventive planning, with uncertainty and human-oversight mechanisms.
5. **Capacity building:** strengthen local expertise in reproducible geospatial analytics, responsible AI, research-data governance, and model auditing.
6. **Transfer assessment:** determine whether the evidence is sufficient for a later validation stage with authorized Peruvian public-safety and local-government stakeholders.

The initial implementation context is Comas, Metropolitan Lima. Findings will not automatically generalize to other Peruvian districts because reporting behavior, urban structure, institutional capacity, and crime patterns differ. Transfer to another location would require local recalibration, validation, governance review, and stakeholder authorization.

## 6. Expected national contribution

The project may contribute to Peru by:

- providing locally generated evidence on district-scale spatiotemporal crime forecasting;
- producing a reproducible and auditable methodological framework rather than a closed black-box system;
- integrating responsible-AI safeguards into public-safety research from the design stage;
- documenting when a model should not be used because of weak calibration, bias, uncertainty, or insufficient data;
- creating research outputs, technical documentation, and training resources that may inform later studies in other Peruvian urban contexts.

The expected public value is better evidence for preventive analysis, not automated enforcement. Funding would enable research and validation; it would not by itself authorize operational deployment.
